#### 1. What is horse racing, you might ask?

It's a type of sport. You have people riding horses on some specific track and try their best to be the first one to cross the finish line. Those people are known as jockeys. And the horses are known as racehorses. But a horse just doesn't come to life and already know how to race. So you have people known as trainers who train the horses. You also have owners who, well, own the horse, and completely decide what they want to do with the horse. In an ideal world, an owner would do what's best for their horses. Read the first clause again.

And as it is the case with any sports, there's ~~gambling~~ betting.

#### 2. Why horse racing, you might ask?

Good question. Allow me to elaborate to you.

I wasn't interested in horse racing growing up. I grew up in Vietnam where horse racing was never a thing, and sports betting was not a thing either. That is to say, I didn't even know about horse racing, let alone having any interest.

It all started in June 2025, when a gacha game called Umamusume Pretty Derby, made by Japanese company Cygames, got released to global server. *Umamusume*, or more properly, *Uma Musume*, is Japanese for *horse girl*. I think you can tell that from the thumbnail already.

You play as a trainer, and you train horse girls whose portrayals were taken directly from well-known real-life racehorses in Japan horseracing history. I'm not gonna dive into the details of gameplay mechanics or gacha mechanics or whatever, I don't exactly play the game anyway.

What I will tell you, is why I even bother mentioning this.

You see, the game does one thing exceptionally well for me. Telling the stories of the real racehorses.

For each and every horse girl in the game, you get to see their personal story. Why they want to race, the achievements they get, the hardships they encounter, how they overcome their fears and insecurities, their interactions and relationship with you, their ~~beloved~~ trainer, and so on and so forth. The devs do a really good job with storytelling, and the VAs really breathe life into their characters, further complementing the storytelling.

Well, I watched the stories. And every time I finished a story, a natural question came to mind: "What was the true story behind this?". It's probably not that natural of a question if you're not particularly inquisitive about everything. But hey, we're talking about me.

So I went deeper into the rabbit hole and read about the stories behind the real-life racehorses. And not just the horses themselves. But also stories about their interactions with the humans in their lives, i.e. their jockeys, trainers and owners. Sometimes their fans even. Some stories were very inspirational. Some were very wholesome (could go really weird but still wholesome). Some were sad. Some were just downright heartbreaking. Yeah, I could get real depressed from that on a bad enough day.

I liked those stories. I remembered each and every one of them.

At some point, I simply no longer could get out of the rabbit hole. Or rather, I refuse to get out.

So there you have it. Your answer to the question of this section.

#### 3. Wait, there's more?

Yes, there's more. I wasn't gonna stop at just that.

But before that, a bit more context.

Each and every country with horseracing has like a billion different races of all levels. And in the horseracing world, there's a concept called Triple Crown, which refers to a set of 3 prestigious races that one could participate in. You win all three, you get the Triple Crown. 

I'm currently in Canada. Canada also has its own Triple Crown. One of them is Breeders' Stakes. Which happened to occur just last week, September 27, at Woodbine Racecourse in Toronto.

For me, this was my rare chance to watch a major horse race in person. So naturally, I folded.

And here is how that day went for me.

#### 4. Breeders' Stakes 2026 - Sunday, September 27, 2026

I'm in Waterloo. And the racecourse is in Toronto. And I don't have a car. And the race starts at 1PM.

Which meant, I had to sacrifice my usual weekend sleep, get my ass on the GO bus at 9AM, and hopefully arrive there at 12PM, so that I may have some food and get myself prepared for the first horserace I'd ever attended.

The trip went pretty smoothly. I had to switch buses a couple of times, but you know, no big deal.

I got to the racecourse! And the first thing I noticed was a giant ass casino area. Huh, should have seen that coming.

I got to the actual racecourse! I walked around and took a bunch of photos. I was excited to see a an actual racecourse in real life. The catch was that I couldn't see my phone screen half the time, so I just prayed that I didn't fumble my photoshoots. Too damn bright, the sky was.

I got to the audience area! Everything looked real nice, and the view of the racetrack was simply extraordinary. But 23°C was feeling like 30°C, because there was literally nothing blocking the sunlight. So I couldn't exactly stay outdoors for very long without going back in every now and then.

I got hungry, so I ordered a burger. T'was mid af.

I finished the burger, so I pulled out my phone to check for pre-race info. That was when I realized one thing. The info currently on display at the racecourse didn't seem to match that of Breeders' Stakes. What was more intriguing was the fact that the upcoming race was labeled "Race 1".

"Wait, there are multiple races today?!", so I thought.

"I definitely must have missed several pages then.", so I thought.

I went to the website and checked it real carefully again.

"Well shit, what do you know?", so I said to myself.

The way things work at a racecourse is that a race day typically has multiple races, the next one usually scheduled 30 minutes away from the previous one. And the time you would see on the website is what's known as first post, the start time of the very first race of that day.

That day, there were 10 races in total. Breeders' Stakes was scheduled to be race 9. Race 1 was at 1PM, so that made Breeders' Stakes land at 5:30PM.

Oh, did I mention the trip between Waterloo and Toronto was 3 hours?

"Oh well, whatever. I have more excuse to stay around for longer anyway.", so I said to myself.

A race comes up. I walk outside to the sunny af weather to observe the race. I find some interesting spot around the area to experience a different point of view. The race begins. I watch closely with all my enthusiasm. The race finishes in 2 minutes or so. I go back inside to cool down from the sunny af weather. I wait for 30 minutes. The next race comes up.

The cycle went on and on like that.

Except, at race 5, I realized another issue.

The immense heat from the sun didn't just affect my ability to stay outside for long.

It also drained my phone's battery. Real. Fricking. Quick.

My phone was fully charged at 9AM. By then at 3PM, it was down to 30%. 

So to make sure I still had a working phone by the time I got home, I decided to shut down all phone services and apps, save for the important ones, and tried my best to not use it unless really necessary.

And I went on with my horseracing spectation.

During the races and the 30-minute intervals between races, I got to explore many things and learned a lot of new stuff.

One, right before the race, jockeys and their horses always do a post parade, which is fancy term for strolling around the track, getting a feel for it and warming up.

Two, the ground view only works if the race takes place at the track right next to the area. If the race takes place at an inner track, go higher up, the view is better there.

Three, different races have different distances, so the starting gates are installed on a truck so that they could easily move around.

Four, there were a lot of boomers there. A LOT.

Five, they were all professional gamblers. That is to say, they were addicts. Man, I've seen those poeple crunching mysterious numbers (also known as statistics) to place their bets and arguing with each other over their bets, defending their decisions as if they were defending their PhD thesis. Actually, never mind that, thesis defense ain't even as heated as what I witnessed.

Six, 90% of the people there, they were there for the gambling. Hence my use of the word "addict" above. A lot of them still talked about the race and the horses, but they were there mostly for the money.

Seven, you can purchase a guide book that gives you all info on all races of that day, as well as how to bet 101. I was mostly interested in the math, not at all in the gambling. Granted, I'm poor, but that was never really a valid excuse for gamblers, was it.

After the whole thing, I went home. There, I realized another issue. 

It was Sunday. The day where 1 billion UW and WLU students were going back to campus.

Let's just say I was lucky enough to not be forced to wait for the next bus 1 fricking hour later. Because the current bus was CHOCK-FULL. Granted, I could stand in the bus instead of sitting. No, who are you kidding? This is a 2-HOUR BUS RIDE, ARE YOU SERIOUS?!

Anyways, I got home. Then it hit me that I got an interview the next day. Guess what? I did my interview while my whole body was still aching from all the constant walking and running at the racecourse. I don't think that interview went well anyway.
